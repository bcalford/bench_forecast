# Ketanji Brown Jackson: Working Profile

## Interpretive method

In statutory cases, Jackson starts with the text and treats congressional intent as the object of interpretation. She writes that the goal "should be 'to give effect to the intent of Congress'" (Fischer, 2024, concurring). She describes the Court's job as implementing "whatever Congress meant" by a phrase (Pugin, 2023, concurring).

She draws on legislative history more than most of her colleagues:
- She calls committee reports "among the best evidence" of statutory meaning and disagrees with Kavanaugh's dissent on that point (Learning Resources, 2026, concurring in part).
- She faults the majority for sidestepping such reports (FS Credit, 2026, dissenting).
- She uses them to read statutory exemptions and review provisions (Catholic Charities, 2025, concurring; Wilkinson, 2024, concurring in the judgment).
- She criticizes "the distorted lens of pure textualism" (Stanley, 2025, dissenting).

Many of her dissents nonetheless rest on plain-text arguments:
- She argued the text "does definitively answer the question" (Brown, 2024, dissenting).
- She called the majority's reading "atextual" (Snyder, 2024, dissenting).
- She said the holding "defies the plain text" (Campos-Chaves, 2024, dissenting).
- She objected to a reading that restored statutory elements Congress had removed (Hansen, 2023, dissenting).

She also gives weight to remedial-construction directives (Medical Marijuana, 2025, concurring).

In constitutional cases, she rejects the Bruen history-and-tradition test and prefers means-end scrutiny (Rahimi, 2024, concurring; Hemani, 2026, concurring; Wolford, 2026, dissenting). She reads the Reconstruction Amendments as a broad "anticaste, antisubordination reset" (Trump v. Barbara, 2026, concurring), and she relies on historical inequality in equal-protection analysis (SFFA, 2023, dissenting).

## Weight given to precedent

Her consistent pattern is to apply precedent she disagrees with while refusing to extend it:
- She joined Rahimi and Hemani while calling Bruen wrongly decided (Rahimi, 2024; Hemani, 2026, both concurring).
- She calls Apprendi wrongly decided but "binding," and declines to extend it (Erlinger, 2024, dissenting).
- She followed Guerrero-Lasprilla despite skepticism, citing the "enhanced force" of statutory stare decisis (Wilkinson, 2024, concurring in the judgment).

She also resists efforts to narrow longstanding precedents:
- Bruton (Samia, 2023, dissenting).
- Garmon (Glacier, 2023, dissenting).
- Thiboutot (Talevski, 2023, majority).
- Pereira and Niz-Chavez (Campos-Chaves, 2024, dissenting).

She objects to reading a precedent as a "magic words" test (Cisco, 2026, partly dissenting).
